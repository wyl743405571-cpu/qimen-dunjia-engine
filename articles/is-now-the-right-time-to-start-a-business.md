# Is Now the Right Time to Start a Business? A Qimen Perspective

"Should I start a business now?" is not one question. It is at least three stacked on top of each
other — and until you separate them, no chart, no advisor, and no spreadsheet can help.

> *Originally published at [QiMenSeek](https://qimenseek.aicreditsapi.com/blog/is-now-the-right-time-to-start-a-business.html). This is a condensed version.*

## Split the question into three

1. **Should I start a business at all?** — an identity and risk-tolerance question. A chart cannot
   answer this; Bazi or honest self-assessment is a better lens.
2. **Is this *specific* venture viable?** — market, product, unit economics. A chart has nothing to
   add; this is diligence work.
3. **Is *now* a good window to launch?** — timing, position, and momentum. **This is where Qimen is
   genuinely useful.**

Most people asking "is now the right time" are actually asking question 1 while pretending it is
question 3. Separate them first.

## What Qimen actually reads for a launch

- **生门 (Life Door)** — whether the venture can sustain itself (resources, cash flow, the ability to
  keep going through a dry spell). This is the single most important gate for a business question.
- **开门 (Open Door)** — the market opening, the formal channel, whether the "door" to customers or
  partners is actually open.
- **The Day Stem's palace** — your own position and what is pressing on it.
- **伏吟 / 空亡** — whether the apparent opening is real or hollow, and whether the period is stagnant.

Read together, these give a structure: *is the market door open, is the business self-sustaining, and
is now a window that moves or a window that stalls?*

## An honest example

A founder asks in late autumn whether to launch a service now. The chart shows 开门 present but 空亡,
生门 weak, and the Day Stem under pressure from a controlling stem. The honest reading is not "don't
launch" — it is **"the door looks open but is hollow right now; the sustaining gate is weak; wait for
the next movement window and use the time to fix the unit economics."** That is a structure, not a
verdict.

## Three checks no chart can do for you

1. **Runway.** How many months can you survive if revenue is zero? A chart cannot see your bank
   balance.
2. **Market evidence.** Conversations, pre-orders, a paid pilot. Structure in the chart is not demand
   in the market.
3. **Personal cost.** What does failure do to your family, health, and optionality? Not in the chart.

If those three are unanswered, the timing question is decoration.

## A practical rhythm for using timing

- Cast **once per decision point**, not every day. Repeatedly re-casting until you get a favourable
  chart is not divination — it is anxiety shopping.
- Use the chart to **schedule the move**, not to replace the diligence.
- If the chart says "wait", define *what must change* before you re-cast. Otherwise "wait" becomes
  indefinite avoidance.
