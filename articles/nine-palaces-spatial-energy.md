# The Nine Palaces: Qimen Dunjia's Spatial Map of a Situation

A Qimen chart is drawn on a 3×3 grid — the **Luoshu (洛书)** nine palaces. It is not decoration. The
grid turns a situation into a map with direction, and direction is an operational variable: which way
to go, where to wait, where the pressure sits.

> *Originally published at [QiMenSeek](https://qimenseek.aicreditsapi.com/blog/nine-palaces-spatial-energy.html). This is a condensed version.*

## The Luoshu frame: one grid, nine territories

```
巽 4   离 9   坤 2
震 3   中 5   兑 7
艮 8   坎 1   乾 6
```

Each palace is a territory with a traditional "governance": a direction, an element, a family
relation, a body area, a life domain. When a symbol lands in a palace, it is speaking inside that
territory — the same 开门 in 乾 reads differently from 开门 in 坎.

## What each palace traditionally governs

| Palace | Direction | Element | Domain (traditional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 坎 1 | North | Water | Career path, hidden matters, danger, flow |
| 坤 2 | Southwest | Earth | Mother, support, land, accumulation |
| 震 3 | East | Wood | Movement, initiation, the eldest son, thunder |
| 巽 4 | Southeast | Wood | Communication, trade, wind, gradual growth |
| 中 5 | Centre | Earth | The pivot; where 天禽 is hosted |
| 乾 6 | Northwest | Metal | Authority, the father, rules, structure |
| 兑 7 | West | Metal | Speech, joy, negotiation, the youngest daughter |
| 艮 8 | Northeast | Earth | Stopping, mountains, transition, the youngest son |
| 离 9 | South | Fire | Visibility, reputation, clarity, the middle daughter |

## How palaces relate

- **Opposite palaces** (坎↔离, 震↔兑, 艮↔坤, 巽↔乾) form a tension axis — the 冲 relation used in
  many judgement methods.
- **Adjacent palaces** share an element or feed each other through the generating cycle — momentum
  passes between them.
- **The centre** (中宫) is special: it hosts 天禽, and in most schools its influence is "lent" to
  another palace rather than read directly.

## Why "space" is an operational variable

Most divination answers *when*. Qimen also answers **where** — because the chart is a map. That is
why classical Qimen is used for 择方 (choosing a direction) as much as for timing: "approach from the
north", "avoid the west this week". Even if you never act on direction, the spatial frame tells you
which *domain* of life the question is really living in.

## A beginner's path through the map

1. Locate your question's palace (Day Stem, or the relevant 用神).
2. Read that palace's domain — is the question really about career, money, speech, or authority?
3. Check the palace opposite — that is often where the obstacle or the counterpart sits.
4. Only then add the star, gate, and god on top.

The grid first, the symbols second. Reading symbols before you know which territory you are standing
in is how beginners get lost.
