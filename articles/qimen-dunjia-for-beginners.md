# Qimen Dunjia for Beginners: A Plain-Language Starting Guide

If you have only ever heard "Qi Men Dun Jia" (奇门遁甲) in a movie or a fortune-teller's shop, this
guide is for you. No mysticism, no "ancient secret" posturing — just what the chart actually is, how
to read it, and what to ignore.

> *Originally published at [QiMenSeek](https://qimenseek.aicreditsapi.com/blog/qimen-dunjia-for-beginners.html). This is a condensed version.*

## One-sentence version

Qimen Dunjia is a **system for asking a specific question about a specific moment** — and reading the
structure of that moment back to you in symbols.

It is not a personality test (that is more Bazi's job) and not a forecast calendar. It is a
*situational* tool: you ask "should I sign this contract this week?", cast for this moment, and read
what the chart says about the opening, the block, and the timing.

## Five symbol layers, each answering one question

1. **Palace (宫)** — *Where* is this happening? (Which domain of life.)
2. **Star (星)** — *What kind* of influence is present? (Support, trouble, momentum, delay.)
3. **Gate (门)** — *Is the path open?* (开门 opening, 生门 livelihood, 死门 closure, 杜门 blockage.)
4. **Deity (神)** — *What flavour / hidden force?* (Authority, entanglement, concealment, connection.)
5. **Stem (干)** — *How are the pieces wired?* (Generating, controlling, combining, clashing.)

A beginner's mistake is to read one layer and stop. The reading is always a **combination**.

## The four-step reading order

1. **Board (盘)** — find the palace your question sits in.
2. **Bureau (局)** — is the overall period expanding or consolidating?
3. **Pattern (格)** — stars, gates, deities in that palace and its opposite.
4. **Response (应)** — stems' relationship, plus timing and direction.

Board → bureau → pattern → response. Read in this order and each step narrows the question.

## A four-step learning path

1. **Learn the grid.** Memorise the nine palaces, their directions, and their elements. That alone
   takes a weekend.
2. **Learn the eight gates.** They carry the most decision-relevant information for beginners.
3. **Read one real chart a day.** Not ten. One question, one chart, one paragraph of conclusion.
4. **Only then add stems and deities.** They are the grammar, not the first words.

## Five traps that make beginners quit

- **Trying to memorise everything first.** You will drown. Learn the grid and gates, then read.
- **Reading symbols without a question.** A chart without a specific question answers nothing.
- **Treating a god or star as a verdict.** "Void in this palace" is information, not doom.
- **Collecting different schools' rules and mixing them.** Pick one 定局 method and stay with it.
- **Asking the chart to decide for you.** It structures; you decide.

## How Qimen relates to other systems

- **Bazi:** a fixed portrait from your birth moment — who you tend to be. Qimen is situational.
- **I Ching:** a binary/six-line oracle for a question. Qimen adds a spatial and timing map.
- **Western astrology:** natal charts read identity and life arc. Qimen reads *this decision now*.

They are not competitors. They answer different questions; use the one that matches the question in
front of you.

the four layers yourself.
