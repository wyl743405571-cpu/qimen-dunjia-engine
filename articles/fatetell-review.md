## Why a framework instead of a feature list

AI divination platforms all look similar at the surface: upload a birth date or a question, get a
reading. The differences that matter are underneath — in whether you can **verify** what you are
being told. So here are the four questions, with a five-minute test for each.

## Question 1 — Is the methodology transparent?

Does the platform say which 定局 method it uses, which 寄宫 convention, whether it is 转盘 or 飞盘?
A platform that will not name its method cannot be compared to any other, and cannot be audited.

**Five-minute test:** look for a method label on the chart itself. If the chart is a pretty picture
with no method annotation, that is your answer.

## Question 2 — Can you test before you pay?

Is there a genuine free tier, or a paywall disguised as a "free trial"? Can you cast a real chart and
see a real reading without entering a card?

**Five-minute test:** cast one chart without an account. If you cannot see the chart itself for free,
the platform is monetising the part that should be deterministic.

## Question 3 — Is the reasoning visible, or only the verdict?

The key question for any AI reading: does it show **which palace each claim came from**? "You are
facing a block" is a verdict. "The Life Door sits in Kun with the Snake, void by 旬空, which reads as
a real opening that is currently hollow" is reasoning you can check.

**Five-minute test:** ask the platform "why" about one specific sentence. If it cannot point to a
symbol, it is generating text, not reading a chart.

## Question 4 — Has the engine been validated, and how?

Ask whether the chart engine has been tested against classical cases, and whether the platform
publishes the results — **including failures**. A perfect scorecard is less trustworthy than a
published list of where the engine and the classical text disagree.

**Five-minute test:** search the platform's site for "accuracy", "validation", or "regression". If
nothing exists, assume no test suite.
